# Privacy

*This is the privacy document from the original, real-data project, written to
be forwarded to the section as-is. This public copy has no real data and no
live deployment — it's kept here because it's the more honest description of
the privacy design than a summary would be.*

**Scaled back, September 2026:** in response to a privacy concern raised by the
school's administration about harvesting data from the card directory, the
site's directory (everyone's photo, contact details, home region, pre-MBA
background) and the face-learning quiz were both removed, and the site was taken
down entirely while this was resolved. The administration confirmed that Calendar
and Social do not depend on that data and are fine to keep running; what's below
describes the site as it now stands — Calendar and Social only, nothing sourced
from the card directory.

## What is on the site

| Data | Source |
|---|---|
| Name and birth month/day | Manually compiled from classmates' own birthday calendar invites — never harvested from the card directory |
| Dietary requirements | A voluntary section survey (not yet sent) |
| Who has met whom | Generated by the meetup allocator |

That's it. Photos, phone numbers, email addresses, home region, pre-MBA employer
and role, education, professional interests, pronouns and fun facts are **not
sent to your browser at all** — not merely hidden by the interface. The build
step (`scripts/encrypt-data.ts`) strips every field down to the table above
before anything is encrypted, so there is nothing extra to find even by opening
DevTools after unlocking.

Only information you have already shared with your classmates directly (e.g. a
birthday calendar invite) is used. Nothing is bought, inferred, or harvested from
the card directory or anywhere else.

**Birth years are never stored.** Only month and day, because the calendar needs
to know when to say happy birthday and does not need to know how old you are.

## Who can see it

The site is behind a **shared section passphrase**. The roster is encrypted with
AES-256-GCM before it is deployed, and decrypted in your browser after you type
the passphrase. The server never holds the key, and someone who downloads the
data file without the passphrase gets ciphertext.

Be clear about what this does and does not protect:

- **It does protect against the open internet.** Search engines cannot index it,
  and a stranger who finds the URL sees a passphrase prompt and nothing else. The
  site also sends `noindex` and ships a `robots.txt` that disallows everything.
- **It does not protect against the section.** It is one shared secret held by
  ninety people. Assume that anyone who has ever been given it still has it.
  Do not put anything on this site you would not put in a section-wide email.

If the section later wants per-person sign-in with revocable access, the
architecture supports it — see the deployment notes in the README.

### The calendar subscribe link is a different, weaker boundary

"Subscribe to section calendar" on Jalendar gives you a URL
(`/calendar.ics?passphrase=…`) with the section passphrase embedded in it. This
is a deliberate exception to the design above, not an oversight:

- **It's a bearer secret.** Anyone who has this specific URL can read event and
  birthday data without ever opening the app or typing anything — the URL itself
  is the credential.
- **It keeps working after you log out**, and after the section passphrase is
  rotated in the app's own unlock screen would normally lock everyone else out —
  this link is independent of that.
- **It's fetched by your calendar provider's servers** (Apple/Google/Outlook),
  not just your own device, on whatever schedule they choose — so the data in it
  transits through their infrastructure repeatedly, not once.

Treat this link exactly like the section passphrase: don't paste it into a
group chat, a public doc, or anywhere outside the section. If a link like this
ever leaks, the fix is the same as a leaked passphrase — rotate
`SECTION_PASSPHRASE` and redeploy; every previously issued subscribe link stops
working at the next refresh.

## The public code repository

The code is open source. The data is not, and never will be.

The public repo contains a **synthetic roster of ninety invented people** so that
anyone can run and develop the site. Real data lives in a separate private
repository that CI reads at deploy time. An automated check (`npm run check:leaks`)
runs on every pull request and fails the build if anything resembling real
personal data appears in the public repo.

Pull requests from forks have no access to the private data or the passphrase.

## Opting out

You can opt out of any part of this, at any time, without giving a reason.

Options, from least to most:

- **Hide your dietary requirements** — your name and birthday stay, the rest goes.
- **Hide your birthday** — you disappear from the calendar.
- **Remove yourself entirely** — you do not appear anywhere on the site.

Opt-outs are applied **at build time**. Suppressed fields are never written into
the encrypted payload, so they are not merely hidden by the interface — they are
not sent to anybody's browser at all.

To opt out, message the section site maintainer or open an issue on the private
data repo. Changes take effect at the next deploy.

## Data we deliberately do not collect

- No analytics, no tracking pixels, no third-party scripts.
- No server-side logs of who looked at whom. There is no server — the site is
  static files on a CDN.
- No leaderboard or scoring of any kind — there's nothing left on the site that
  would produce one.
